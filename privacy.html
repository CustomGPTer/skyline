Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026. This policy covers the Skyline app for Android (package com.skyline.weather) and this website. Skyline is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • There is no account, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. The developer receives nothing from the app.
  • To get a forecast, Skyline asks Open-Meteo directly, which sees your IP address and the rounded position of the place.
  • Radar, maps and Met Office warnings are off until you switch them on.
  • Your places and settings stay on your phone, and phone backups leave them out.

Who the app talks to, and when

ServiceWhat it receivesWhen
Open-Meteo place searchYour IP address and the town you typedWhen you press Search
Open-Meteo forecastsYour IP address and the rounded position of the placeWhen a forecast is opened or refreshed, and for background checks you switch on
Open-Meteo air quality and pollenYour IP address and the rounded positionOn by default; can be switched off
RainViewer and OpenStreetMapYour IP address and the map area you look atOnly after you switch maps on, while the radar is open
Met OfficeYour IP address and a request for the UK-wide feed (no position)Only after you switch warnings on, when you open them
GitHubYour IP address and a request for the newest Skyline releaseOnly when you tap Check for updates

Each service handles what it receives under its own policy. Open-Meteo says its troubleshooting logs may contain positions and are deleted after 90 days. There is no Skyline server, so nothing is sent to the developer.

Location

Town search needs no permission. Use approximate location asks Android for a rough position once, when you tap it. Skyline rounds it to about 2 km and saves it as a fixed place. It never asks for precise or background location, and background checks use your saved places, not your device's position.

What stays on your phone

Your places, settings and the latest forecasts are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Android's cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off for Skyline. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

What the app can reach

Internet access, approximate location (only when you ask), notifications (only for alerts you switch on), starting after a restart (to keep your background checks), and installing packages (only to hand a downloaded, verified Skyline update to Android, which asks you to confirm). No camera, microphone, contacts, storage or precise-location access.

Exports and sharing

Export settings saves a file with your places and preferences where you choose. Keep it private: it lists your places. Share forecast sends a short text through your phone's share menu. Neither happens unless you ask.

Deleting your data

Settings, Delete all app data removes your places, settings, forecasts and map cache, and stops background checks. It cannot recall a settings file you exported, or requests a service has already received.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies and no analytics. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, for security, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Skyline collects nothing from anyone, and has nothing in it unsuitable for children.
